# IBM PC Operating System Market

## Description

In 1980, IBM—the dominant force in computing—decided to enter the personal computer market. This "Project Chess" represented a massive market opportunity:

### Problem Statement

IBM needed an operating system for their upcoming PC but lacked internal capability to build one quickly. Their first choice, Digital Research (CP/M), failed to close a deal due to Gary Kildall's absence and contract disputes.

### Evidence

- IBM approaching third-party vendors signaled urgency
- IBM's brand would legitimize the PC market
- CP/M negotiations failing created an opening
- IBM's tight timeline (ship in 12 months) favored fast movers

### Market Size

- IBM's distribution could reach every business in America
- PC market projected to grow 10x in 5 years
- Enterprise credibility would open doors competitors couldn't

## Strategic Alignment

- [x] Builds **Scale Economies** — OS licensing scales infinitely
- [x] Strengthens **Network Effects** — More PCs = more software = more PCs
- [x] Enables **Counter-Positioning** — Hardware vendors can't also control OS
- [x] Increases **Switching Costs** — Applications lock users to the OS
- [ ] Enhances **Brand**
- [x] Secures **Cornered Resource** — Non-exclusive license allows clones

## Outcome

Microsoft seized this opportunity through ODR-005 (Partner with IBM) and ODR-006 (Buy QDOS), becoming the standard OS provider for the entire PC industry—not just IBM.

**Result**: MS-DOS became the foundation of Microsoft's dominance for the next 15 years.
